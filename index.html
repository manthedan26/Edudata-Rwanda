Library Official REB books and national exam past papers

Books are published by the Rwanda Education Board (REB) and open on the official REB eLearning site. Past papers open the official NESA folders. This platform links to the official sources and does not copy their files. Some resources may ask for a free REB account.

Who goes to school, and where? Results are calculated from NISR’s public-use census file (about 1.3 million people), weighted with the population weight. Only aggregated results are shown; no individual is identified.

How to read this

  • Attendance = share of children aged 6 to 17 who are currently attending school (any level).
  • Adults 25+ = share with lower secondary, upper secondary or tertiary education as their highest level.
  • Reading and writing = can read and write with understanding in at least one language, ages 15+.
  • Sample: NISR’s public-use file is a 10% equal-probability sample of private households (people in institutions are not included). Percentages are weighted with the population weight. Small differences between districts may not be meaningful. These are not official NISR statistics.
  • Checked against NISR: our attendance for ages 6–11 (89.4%) and 12–17 (73.2%), and literacy for ages 15+ (78.8%), match or nearly match NISR’s published education report.
  • Source: NISR, Rwanda Population and Housing Census 2022, microdata.statistics.gov.rw (study RWA-NISR-RPHC-2022-v0.1). Method: tools/census_education.py.

Methods and tools

Python (pandas) for the census results; HTML, CSS and JavaScript for this website; GitHub for the code and hosting; Google Forms for user feedback.

Data principle

A district with lower attendance or fewer schools does not prove a problem. The platform shows patterns that may need further investigation.

Methodology and data quality

  • School figures (schools, learners, owner, status, disability, internet) come only from the MINEDUC Education Statistical Yearbook 2024/2025.
  • Census 2022: NISR public-use microdata, a 10% equal-probability sample of private households (institutions excluded), weighted with the population weight. School age is 6 to 17, as in NISR’s report.
  • Validation: our attendance for ages 6-11 (89.4%) and 12-17 (73.2%) and literacy for ages 15+ (78.8%) match or nearly match NISR’s published report.
  • Limits: small differences between districts may not be meaningful. Results are not official NISR statistics, and only aggregated results are published.
  • Reproducible: tools/census_education.py rebuilds the district results from the NISR file.

About Manthedan · Edudata-rwanda

What is Edudata-rwanda?

This platform is built by Manthedan to make education information in Rwanda easier to discover, understand and access: official REB books, national exam past papers and school data, with an AI assistant that speaks Kinyarwanda, English and French.

National priorities: Vision 2050 and NST2

Rwanda’s Vision 2050 aims for upper-middle income by 2035 and high income by 2050. The second National Strategy for Transformation (NST2, 2024–2029) turns that vision into five-year action through three pillars: Economic Transformation, Social Transformation (which includes education) and Transformational Governance. One NST2 priority is improving the quality and market relevance of education.

  • Learning resources: official REB books and NESA past papers in one place for learners and teachers.
  • Evidence for planning: NISR Census 2022 indicators by district and MINEDUC national figures show where school attendance and literacy are lower.
  • Digital access: a free website that works on phones, in Kinyarwanda, English and French.

Edudata-rwanda supports these priorities; it is not an official NST2 monitoring tool and does not measure the quality of teaching. Sources: Government of Rwanda, NST2 (2024–2029); Office of the Prime Minister.
